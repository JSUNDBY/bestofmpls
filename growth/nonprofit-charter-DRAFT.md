# Carved-out public-good project — charter  [DRAFT / OPTIONAL, 6-12 mo play]

This is the one-page charter that makes grant money possible. The logic: a
for-profit guide can't take charitable money, but a genuine public-good arts /
community-documentation project CAN, under a fiscal sponsor. This project must
be editorially distinct from the commercial guide and its ad sales. Only pursue
this if it's real to you, funders can smell a shell.

## Working name
**The Twin Cities Culture Project** (or your better name)

## Mission (the charitable purpose)
To document and widen public access to the everyday cultural life of Minneapolis
and St. Paul, especially the small, immigrant-owned, and neighborhood places and
events that no glossy outlet covers, so that more people can find and support
them. Free to the public, no paywall, no advertising in this body of work.

## What it actually produces
- A free, ongoing record of the metro's cultural life: neighborhood guides,
  artist and small-business profiles, an open events/lectures calendar, oral
  histories of places we lose ("Departed").
- Particular focus on under-covered communities and the Cultural Districts.
- Behind-the-camera short documentary video of the people behind the food and
  the art (your craft, your edge).

## Why it's distinct from bestofmpls.com (the for-profit guide)
- No featured/paid placements, no sponsorships, no affiliate links in this work.
- Editorial selection is about cultural value and access, not commerce.
- The for-profit guide may link to it; the funded project never sells anything.
- Clean separation of money: sponsored funds pay for documentation work
  (your time, production, maybe contributor stipends), not the guide's growth.

## Who it serves
The public, free. Especially residents looking to discover and support local and
immigrant-owned culture, and the small businesses/artists who get documented.

## How it gets funded
- **Fiscal sponsor:** Springboard for the Arts Incubator (rolling, monthly).
- **Then eligible for:** MRAC (Next Step / Creative Connections), foundation arts
  money, and a cleaner conversation with Arts & Cultural Affairs about the
  Cultural Districts Fund for a district-scoped chapter.
- **Also (no sponsor needed):** MSAB Creative Individuals, applied as an artist.

## Honest test before you build this
Ask Carl's question: do you actually want to run a documented public-good arts
project, or are you reaching for the money? If it's real and it excites you, this
is a legitimate and fundable second track. If it's just a wrapper to get a grant,
skip it, it won't survive a funder conversation and it'll drain you. The services
and listings income (SALES-KIT.md) is the honest fast money and needs none of this.
